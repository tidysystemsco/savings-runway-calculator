How to Find the Subscriptions You Forgot You're Paying For

Subscription costs are designed to be forgettable — small, recurring, automatic. That's exactly why they're worth checking on purpose every so often.

The five-minute audit

Pull up your bank and credit card statements from the last 60 days. Scan for anything recurring — same amount, roughly same date each month. For each one, ask a single question: did I actively use this in the last 30 days? Not "might I use it eventually" — actively used.

This audit commonly turns up $30-60/month in subscriptions that were still being charged after the person stopped using them — found in about five minutes, with no extra income required.

Why this works better than "just be more careful"

The problem was never carelessness — it's that recurring charges are invisible by design. You don't get a decision point each month the way you do with a one-time purchase; the charge just happens. A periodic audit re-creates that decision point on your terms instead of the subscription's.

Make it a recurring check, not a one-time fix

New subscriptions creep back in — a free trial that converts, a family member's shared plan, an app you needed for one project. Doing this same five-minute check once a quarter keeps the list honest without turning into a full-time chore.
