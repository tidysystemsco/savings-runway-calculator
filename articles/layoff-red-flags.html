4 Signs You're One Layoff Away From Financial Disaster

Income level says very little about how exposed you actually are to a job loss. These four signs matter far more than the size of the paycheck.

1. No emergency fund

Without one, a layoff immediately becomes a countdown to debt rather than a manageable transition — even with a strong resume and good prospects.

2. Only one income stream

A single household income source means a single point of failure. This isn't a call to necessarily add another job, just an honest acknowledgment of how much is riding on one source continuing uninterrupted.

3. Spending scaled to gross pay, not net

Budgeting off the number before taxes and deductions rather than what actually lands in the bank account is one of the most common — and least visible — ways people end up with less margin than they think they have.

4. No updated resume or network in over a year

This one is about response time, not money. A stale resume and a cold network can turn a job search that should take a few weeks into one that takes several months, extending exactly the period when the other three factors matter most.

Any one of these on its own is a minor gap. All four together is a pattern — and it's a pattern that's fixable well before a layoff ever happens.
